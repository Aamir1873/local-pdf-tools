Why it matters

Why not upload PDFs to free PDF websites?

Free websites that merge, split or compress PDFs work by uploading your file to their computers. Most people never think about what happens to the file next.

Your file leaves your control

Once it's uploaded, you can't see who can open it, how long it's kept, or where in the world it's stored. A promise to "delete after one hour" is only a promise.

PDFs hold the most private papers

Passports, payslips, bank statements, contracts, medical letters, tax forms and CVs. These are exactly the files people split and merge most.

At work, it can break the rules

Uploading a file with other people's personal data, such as clients or staff, to a service your organisation hasn't approved can break GDPR and your company's policy. Under GDPR, a service that handles personal data for you needs a written agreement (Article 28).

Fake buttons and add-ons

Some sites are covered in ads with their own "Download" buttons, or push you to install an extension or an app. That's how unwanted software gets onto computers.

Lookalike sites

Copycat sites use names and designs that look like well-known tools. A search ad at the top of the results isn't proof that a site is genuine.

The fix is simple

Do it on your own device. Your computer and phone can already do most PDF jobs, and Local PDF Tools does the rest without uploading anything.

Use what you already have

How to merge and split PDFs with the tools on your device

Menu names can differ slightly between system versions. If a step doesn't match what you see, use Local PDF Tools instead.

Mac Mac: Preview and Finder

Combine PDFs

  1. Open the first PDF in Preview.
  2. Choose View → Thumbnails to show the sidebar.
  3. Drag the other PDFs from Finder into the sidebar, where you want them.
  4. Choose File → Export as PDF and save under a new name.

Quicker: select the files in Finder, Control-click, then Quick Actions → Create PDF. Files are combined in name order, so number them first (01, 02…).

Take pages out

  1. Open the PDF in Preview and show Thumbnails.
  2. Select the pages you want (Command-click for several).
  3. Drag them to the desktop. This makes a new PDF with just those pages.

Or choose File → Print, set the page range, then PDF → Save as PDF.

Delete, reorder or rotate

  1. In the Thumbnails sidebar, select a page.
  2. Press Delete to remove it, drag it to move it, or use Tools → Rotate Left/Right.
  3. Save a copy with File → Export as PDF.

Win Windows: Microsoft Edge

Take pages out

  1. Open the PDF in Microsoft Edge (right-click → Open with → Edge).
  2. Press Ctrl + P to print.
  3. Set the printer to Save as PDF or Microsoft Print to PDF.
  4. Under Pages, type the pages you want, such as 2-5, then save.

Combine PDFs

Windows doesn't have a built-in way to combine PDFs. Use Local PDF Tools: open the file in Edge or Chrome and choose Merge. It works offline and nothing is uploaded.

Avoid "free PDF converter" downloads from ad-heavy sites. If your workplace has an approved PDF app, use that.

Delete, reorder or rotate

Use Local PDF Tools → Pages. For deleting pages, you can also print only the pages you want to keep (see "Take pages out").

iOS iPhone and iPad: the Files app

Combine PDFs

  1. Open Files and go to the folder with your PDFs.
  2. Tap ••• (top right) → Select, then tap the files.
  3. Tap ••• (bottom right) → Create PDF.

The pages follow the file names' order, so rename them 01, 02… first.

Take pages out

  1. Long-press the PDF → Duplicate, and open the copy.
  2. Show the page thumbnails (swipe in from the left edge, or tap the sidebar button).
  3. Long-press each page you don't want → Delete.

Rotate or add pages

In the same thumbnails view, long-press a page for Rotate Left/Right, Insert Blank Page or Insert from File.

And Android

Take pages out

  1. Open the PDF in the Files or Drive app, or in Chrome.
  2. Choose Print from the menu.
  3. Pick Save as PDF, set the pages, and save.

Combine PDFs

Android doesn't have a built-in way to combine PDFs. Use Local PDF Tools on a computer. If you use an app, check its Data safety section in Google Play first, and pick one that works without uploading.

Check before you install

Many "PDF" apps upload files to a server or show heavy ads. Read reviews, check the developer, and check what data it collects.

Cr Chromebook

Take pages out

  1. Open the PDF in Chrome.
  2. Press Ctrl + P, choose Save as PDF.
  3. Set Pages to a custom range and save.

Everything else

Open Local PDF Tools from the Files app. It opens in Chrome and works offline.

Lnx Linux: terminal

Combine

qpdf --empty --pages a.pdf b.pdf -- merged.pdf

Or with poppler-utils: pdfunite a.pdf b.pdf merged.pdf

Take pages out

qpdf in.pdf --pages . 2-5 -- pages-2-5.pdf

One file per page: pdfseparate in.pdf page-%d.pdf

Rotate

qpdf in.pdf out.pdf --rotate=+90:1

Rotates page 1 by 90° clockwise.

If you must use a website

A 6-point check before you upload anything

  1. Is it a file you'd be fine seeing in public? If it has an ID, money, health, HR or client details, don't upload it. Do it on your device instead.
  2. Who runs the site? Look for a company name and address. No owner, no upload.
  3. What does the privacy policy say? How long files are kept, where they're processed, and whether they're used for anything else, such as training AI.
  4. Is it approved at work? For work files, use only tools your organisation approves. A service that handles personal data needs a processor agreement.
  5. Does it push downloads? Leave if it asks you to install an extension, an app or a "converter" to finish.
  6. Is it the real address? Type the address yourself instead of clicking an ad, and check the spelling.

Already uploaded something?

What to do now

1. Ask for deletion

Use the site's delete option if it has one. Otherwise email it and ask it to delete your file. Under GDPR, you can ask for your personal data to be erased (Article 17).

2. Tell your workplace

If the file had other people's personal data, tell your manager or data protection officer today. Your organisation may have to report a breach within 72 hours.

3. Protect yourself

If it had passwords, account numbers or ID details, change those passwords and watch your accounts for anything you don't recognise.

Keep it handy

Keep Local PDF Tools one click away

First, move Local PDF Tools.html out of your Downloads folder and into Documents, so it doesn't get cleaned up by mistake. Then add a shortcut.

Mac Mac

In the Dock

  1. Open your Documents folder in Finder.
  2. Drag Local PDF Tools.html to the right side of the Dock, next to the Trash.
  3. Click it any time to open the tool.

On the desktop

  1. Control-click the file and choose Make Alias.
  2. Drag the alias to your desktop and rename it "PDF Tools".

In your browser

  1. Open the tool.
  2. Press Command-D to bookmark it.
  3. Save it to Favorites (Safari) or the Bookmarks bar (Chrome).

Win Windows

On the desktop

  1. Open your Documents folder.
  2. Right-click Local PDF Tools.html, then Show more options → Send to → Desktop (create shortcut).
  3. Rename the shortcut "PDF Tools".

In your browser

  1. Open the tool in Edge or Chrome.
  2. Press Ctrl + D and save it to the Favorites bar or Bookmarks bar.
  3. Press Ctrl + Shift + B if you can't see the bar.

Opens in the wrong app?

Right-click the file, choose Open with, and pick Edge, Chrome or Firefox.

More Chromebook, Linux, phones and tablets

Chromebook

  1. Move the file to My files.
  2. Open it, press Ctrl + D and save it to the Bookmarks bar.

Linux

Keep the file in your home folder and bookmark it in your browser with Ctrl + D.

Don't just trust us

How to check that Local PDF Tools stays offline

Turn off the internet

Switch off Wi-Fi or turn on airplane mode, then open the file and merge something. It still works, because everything runs on your device.

It blocks itself

The file has a built-in security rule (a Content Security Policy) that stops it from connecting to any website, even if it wanted to.

Look for yourself

Open your browser's developer tools (F12 or Option-Command-I), go to the Network tab and use the tool. You'll see no uploads.
